# Subtitle sync

Subtitles in text can be synced to a film's speech: an SRT, ASS or SSA file added to the film, or a subtitle track the film already has in text (SRT, ASS, SSA, timed text, WebVTT). ASS stays ASS, its styles, signs and songs kept (see [ASS and SSA](#ass-and-ssa)). **Sync to speech**, a key beside the subtitle track's flags in the tracks card, gives every line the moment its words are spoken. It works line by line, not by moving the whole file, so a file whose lines are off in different directions (one 1.5 s late, the next 2 s early) comes out right, and it needs no other subtitles to compare with: the film's own sound is the reference.

It is an optional download (Settings › Subtitle sync), made only when the user asks for it. Until then the button is locked and points there. Once downloaded it runs offline: the models are loaded from disk (`HF_HUB_OFFLINE`), and nothing leaves the PC.

## What it does

`src-tauri/sync/siphon_sync.py`, run by `src-tauri/src/subsync/` in a private Python:

1. **The subtitles**, when they are a track of the film's own, are taken out of it first to a file in `subsync\work\`, by Siphon's FFmpeg (`SubSync::take_out`): an ASS or SSA track copied as it is (`styled` asks ffprobe which it is), anything else as SRT. It reads the whole film for them, as a track's packets are spread all through it: seconds on an SSD. The file is deleted as soon as the sync has read it.
2. **The sound it listens to** (`speechTrack` in `draft.ts`): of the film's own sound, one in a language the aligner times (see Limits), else one with no language tag, else any, which is then refused with the reason; never commentary while there is other sound; among those, the one that plays first, else the first kept, else the first. Every sound track runs on the film's clock, so a Greek dub that plays first doesn't keep the English beside it from being heard, kept or not; the button says which it listens to ("listening to the English sound").
3. **The dialogue** is pulled out of that sound track by Siphon's FFmpeg: the centre channel of a 5.1 or 7.1 mix, where films put speech, away from most music; both channels of stereo. 16 kHz mono.
4. **Where someone speaks**: Silero VAD (MIT). It runs over 32 stretches of the film at once, each first hearing the 60 s before it so its memory has settled as if it had heard everything before; its own rules then turn its probabilities into speech regions (from 0.35, silence below 0.20 for 250 ms, 150 ms margins). Measured on Sicario: 1,081 of 1,082 regions the same as one long pass, in 11 s instead of 48.
5. **What is said, word by word**: Qwen3-ASR 1.7B (Apache-2.0) writes it down, chunks of up to 30 s cut in silences, 16 at a time; Qwen3-ForcedAligner 0.6B (Apache-2.0) times every word. Its published accuracy is 25-45 ms on average against about 130 ms for WhisperX's method. A scene in another language is written down in that language (Sicario's Spanish was).
6. **Phrases**: the words cut at pauses of 0.3 s and at punctuation the recogniser wrote.
7. **A rough whole-film fit** places each line near its speech first: lines on screen against speech, every 10 ms, by FFT cross-correlation, over seven frame-rate ratios (25 / 23.976 and the like), then a straight line through the offsets of 10-minute stretches for a slow drift, each looked for within 20 s of the whole film's offset. Until 2026-11-27 they were looked for within 20 s of none, which put a file more than about 20 s off as a whole back where it was: 60 s late, not one of Sicario's lines was paired, and the file was written as it came. No line is paired with speech more than 7 s from where this puts it.
8. **Pairing by meaning**: BGE-M3 (MIT) compares every line, and every run of 2-3 lines, with every nearby run of phrases, across languages (Greek lines against English speech). One pass of dynamic programming picks the pairing that agrees best overall, in order. A line may take several phrases; lines may share one phrase, which is then cut where each part best means what its line says; speech no line takes (an untranslated "Hmm") and lines no speech matches (a sign, a credit) stay unpaired.
9. **Times**: a paired line starts 0.04 s before its first word and stays 0.45 s after its last, lengthened to be readable (20 characters a second) without running into the next line. A line too quick to read at all may start up to 0.25 s early, taken from the line before.
10. **What pairing missed**: lines and speech both left unpaired between the same two paired lines are each other (a name the recogniser misheard: "Wan" heard as "One"). Words the aligner stacked on one instant are re-timed from the words around them, and a line whose first word the recogniser missed starts where the voice detector heard speech begin. Anything still unplaced takes the shift of the lines around it. A line that lands before the film starts (a credit nobody speaks, in a file that ran late) starts with the film instead, in its order and spacing, squeezed into the time before the next line if it must be: until 2026-11-27 it was written as 0:00 to 0:00, and never showed.
11. **A sync that matched nothing is refused** (*None of the lines matched what is said in the film. The subtitles may be for another film.*): with no line paired by its words, every time would be a guess. Until 2026-11-27 such a file was written anyway, as *Synced*.

The synced copy is written in UTF-8 in `subsync\work\`, as SRT, or as ASS or SSA when that is what was synced. A file added is swapped for it in the draft (`withSynced` in `draft.ts`), and **Use the file's own timing** puts the file back. A track of the film's own stays where it is in the draft (`withTrackSynced`, `Draft.syncs`), and the copy takes that track's lines from the synced file instead of the film (`editOf`), as it takes a file added; **Use the track's own timing** undoes it. Either way every choice made of the track (language, name, flags, place) is kept, and the row says **Synced**. The key itself widens into the sync's progress while it runs (its stage in a few words, how far, ✕ to stop), and once done says **Synced**, what was paired in its hint, with the undo beside it (`SyncKey.tsx`).

## ASS and SSA

ASS (Advanced SubStation Alpha; SSA is its older form) holds more than lines and times: styles (fonts, colours, outlines, where a line sits), override tags in `{…}` (`\pos`, `\fad`, `\i1`), and besides dialogue, signs (on-screen text translated, pinned to the picture), songs (karaoke, timed by the syllable from the line's start), drawings, and comments. It is what fansubs and many releases use, often inside an MKV with its fonts attached. Converted to SRT, all of that would be lost, so it is synced as ASS (`read_ass`, `write_ass` in `siphon_sync.py`):

- **The events are read by the `Format:` line** of `[Events]`, whatever its fields' order; a `Dialogue:` or `Comment:` row that doesn't fit it is left alone.
- **Only dialogue is paired with speech.** An event is not dialogue when it is a comment, has karaoke syllables (`\k`, `\kf`, `\ko`), is pinned, moving or drawn (`\pos`, `\move`, `\org`, `\clip`, `\p1`), has an effect (`Banner;`, `Scroll up;`), has no words, or has a style named, by a word of it, as signs, titles, notes, an opening or ending, a song, lyrics, karaoke, romaji or kanji ("Signs", "TS", "OP1 Romaji", "ED-Kanji"). A line at the top of the screen (`\an8`) is still dialogue.
- **A line drawn on several layers is one line** (a glow under it, an outline over it: the same times and the same words, tags aside), and its layers get the same new times. The lines are put in time order for the pairing, as SRT's are; a typesetter's file often lists signs after all the dialogue.
- **Signs, songs and drawings move with the scene around them**, keeping their length: by the median shift of the dialogue lines placed by their words within a minute of them (or of the nearest nine). One that would start before the film does starts with it, keeping its length. Not by their next neighbours alone: in a file out of sync each line has its own error, up to 2 s either way, which a sign would take on (tried: up to 1.3 s off that way, 0.9 s by the median). So a sign stays on its shot, and karaoke syllables on the beat. **Comments** are left as they are.
- **Only Start and End are rewritten**, in the file's own precision (centiseconds): every other byte stays, the script info, styles, text, tags, row order, line endings and a UTF-8 byte-order mark among them.
- **Kept as ASS when saved**: a file added is added as ASS, as before; a track of the film's own is taken from the synced file in its place, and the film's fonts stay attached. An MP4 copy still turns ASS into timed text, as for any ASS it is given.

**The tracks card can be put away while it syncs.** The result changes the film's choices as they are when it arrives (`later` in `TracksPanel.tsx`, a `DraftChange` given as a function), only while they are still that film's; until 2026-11-26 it was applied to a copy of them kept from when the card was last open, so a choice made after reopening the card mid-sync was undone when the sync landed. Checked in the dev build: put away mid-sync and opened again after, the track was **Synced**; opened again mid-sync and another track marked Forced, both the mark and the sync were kept. **Closing the film** (or the list of files it is in) stops its syncs, the one running and those waiting (`useSyncsLeaveWith`): its choices of tracks go with it, so nothing would take what they make. While a film's subtitles sync or wait to, its copy can't be saved (*Syncing its subtitles…*), as it would be saved with their old timing.

## A season, and taking turns

Syncs take turns, one at a time, in the order asked (`useSubSync` in `src/stores/subsync.ts`). One sync holds 8.35 GB of the graphics card at its peak (PyTorch's own count, measured 2026-11-26 on a Korra episode; it keeps 10.7 GB on a 16 GB card, as PyTorch keeps what it freed for reuse) and CUDA takes about half a gigabyte more, so two at once wouldn't fit even a 16 GB card: both would crawl on memory NVIDIA's driver lends from the PC, or fail. One after another, each starts the moment the last ends, its models loaded afresh and its memory given back between files, so a game started meanwhile has the card between two episodes.

- **A season's subtitles are synced together.** In a season's tracks card, each row of subtitles that can be synced (the episodes' own in text, or files added to them: *Greek · Added*) has **Sync to speech**, which asks for the row's subtitles in every file at once, each listening to its own episode's sound (`rowSyncs` in `season.ts`, `useRowSync` in `SeasonTracks.tsx`). The row's key then names the episode syncing and how far the whole row has got, with ✕ to stop them all; under the row, at what stage, and how many are synced. When they are done the row says *Synced to the speech* and the key **Synced**, with a way back to their own timing in every file; some failed or left out, *2 of 3 synced* and **Sync 1 more**, with why each failed, the same reason said once for all the episodes it held up. In the list, each episode says *Syncing 45%* or *Waits to sync*, and **Save** waits for them (*Syncing their subtitles… 3 to go*).
- **One film's own button queues too.** Pressed while another file syncs, it waits its turn (the key says *Waits its turn · 2 ahead*), with ✕ to take it out of the queue alone. Each sync, asked from the season's row or the episode's own card, is the same one: an episode's row in the list shows it running or waiting either way.
- **Room on the graphics card.** Before each sync starts, `nvidia-smi` is asked how much of the card is free (`wait_for_card` in `subsync/mod.rs`). Under 9 GB (on a card of less than 10 GB, all but 1 GB, which Windows keeps), it waits, saying *Waiting for room on the graphics card*, and looks again every 2 seconds, until there is room or it is stopped; the log says how much was free. A card that can't be asked is taken as free.
- **Checked in the dev build** (2026-11-26, on copies of Korra S01E01-03 with their Greek SRT scrambled as below and added by name): the three synced one after another, about 33 s each with no gap between them, from a median 1.8-2.0 s off to 0.00 s, 94-96% of lines within 0.2 s of where the sync puts each episode's own Greek track; then the episodes' own tracks, asked while the first three ran, waited and synced after them. E01's own track, synced through the queue, came out byte for byte as the same sync run alone. With 6 GB of the card held by another program (6.9 GB free), the sync waited, and went on within 2 s of it letting go. Taken out of the queue alone, E03 was skipped and the row said *2 of 3 synced*; stopped mid-batch, and with the list closed mid-sync, the sync's process ended and nothing was left in `subsync\work\`. Saved, each copy held the synced lines, the original's SHA-256 unchanged.

**Only what is used is kept.** A synced copy is needed only between the sync and the copy that saves it, and for saving again: the copy is made by FFmpeg from files, so the synced lines wait in one. It stays while a choice of tracks holds it (`useSyncedInUse`, in the single file's pane and the files list) or an unfinished copy may read it again on Resume or Try again (one queued, running, paused or failed in Activity), and is deleted as soon as neither does: undone, the film closed, the failed copy taken out of Activity (`sync_keep`, `SubSync::keep_only`, a moment after any of these; never while a sync runs, so a copy just made isn't taken before its draft has it). A sync that fails or is stopped leaves nothing. When Siphon closes, `subsync\work\` is emptied, and again at launch in case it didn't close, but for the synced copies a paused or failed copy of tracks reads (`SubSync::keep_work`, `DownloadManager::inputs_kept`): a film's choices of tracks aren't kept across a restart, but Activity's Resume and Try again are. Until 2026-11-27 those went too, and the copy couldn't be resumed. Checked in the dev build: the track taken out was gone the moment its sync ended; the synced copy went within a second of **Use the track's own timing**; it stayed through saving while the film was open, and through closing the film while a failed copy of it was in Activity, and went when that copy was taken out; one in use when Siphon closed was gone, and five left from before were gone at launch.

## The download

`<app-local-data>\subsync\`, nothing outside it (and `work\`, synced copies while they are used, see above):

| Part | Download | On disk |
|---|---|---|
| uv 0.13.10 (Astral, checked against its published SHA-256) | 22 MB | 50 MB |
| Python 3.14, private: `--no-registry --no-bin`, uv told to use only its own (`UV_PYTHON_PREFERENCE=only-managed`), so the user's own Python is neither used nor touched | 30 MB | 130 MB |
| PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 12.8, and every other package at the version tested (`sync/requirements.txt`, from `uv pip freeze`) | 3.3 GB | 4.9 GB |
| Qwen3-ASR 1.7B, Qwen3-ForcedAligner 0.6B, BGE-M3 at pinned revisions; BGE-M3 stored at half precision, which is how it runs | 8.8 GB | 7.3 GB |

uv's download cache is deleted once the packages are copied out of it. A download that stops carries on where it stopped (each model has a `.complete` mark); `ready.json`, written last, says the whole is there, and its `VERSION` makes an older install be made again when the packages or models change. **Remove** in Settings deletes the folder.

**How fast it downloads** (measured 2026-11-26, on the owner's line of about 55-63 MiB/s): a first install took 4 min 40 s for 12.1 GB. PyTorch (download.pytorch.org) and the models (Hugging Face's CDN) each came at the line's full speed, and four connections at once were no faster than one, so there is no quicker source: PyTorch's CUDA builds for Windows are only on its own index (PyPI's are for the processor alone), and Hugging Face is the models' home. About 65 s of it is not downloading: installing the packages (26 s) and making BGE-M3 half size (36 s). Starting the models while PyTorch installs, and converting BGE-M3 while the others download, would save about a minute; left as it is (the owner's call), since it is fast already and the install works. Hugging Face's own parallel downloader, `hf_xet`, is not installed: `huggingface_hub` asks for it only where the processor is named `amd64` in lower case, and Windows says `AMD64`. It could help on lines faster than one connection gets, which couldn't be measured here; it would also keep a chunk cache, to be turned off (`HF_XET_CHUNK_CACHE_SIZE_BYTES=0`).

The download is offered only where `nvidia-smi` lists an NVIDIA card with 8 GB or more; the models run in bfloat16 on CUDA.

## Measured

On the owner's PC (RTX 5080, 16 GB), 2026-11-26.

- **Sicario** (2 h 1 min, 7.1 English with Spanish scenes; a Greek SRT from another release that is about 1 s late at the start and 2 s at the end, and off line by line). The owner reported seven lines by ear (9:21, 10:23-10:26, 11:50, 12:09, 12:17, 35:15, 59:57); all came out where they were heard, and the owner judged the whole file right. 903 lines: 868 paired by their words, 35 placed between them. **1 min 46 s** in all, with the models loaded.
- **Korra S02E08** (24 min, a correctly timed Greek SRT scrambled on purpose: a 1.7 s delay, a slow drift, and every line moved by up to ±2 s): from a median 2.1 s off to **0.12 s**; 84% of lines within 0.2 s and 89% within 0.5 s of the original timing, in 27 s. Of the 11 lines still a second or more apart, several are the original's own errors (it is fan-made: "Ράβα!" is spoken at 13:50.56, synced to 13:50.52, the original says 13:52.89) and five are credits that are never spoken.
- Speeding it up was checked against the version the owner approved: every one of the 903 lines within 0.1 s of it.
- **A track of the film's own** (Korra S01E01, 24 min): its Greek SRT track scrambled as above, the lines kept in their order, and muxed back in its place; the sync listened to the English surround, the Greek dub playing first. Synced in 36 s, 334 of 338 lines by their words. Against where the sync puts the unscrambled track: median 0.00 s, 97% of lines within 0.2 s. Synced as an added file instead, the same scrambled lines came out within 0.2 s of that, every one. Against the track's own timing the median was 0.34 s, the sync 0.24 s later on the whole, and the unscrambled track synced the same: it was timed to the Greek dub and starts its lines early, so it was not the measure. Saved, the copy held the six tracks in their order, the Greek one with the synced lines and its choices, the others' packets and the chapters identical, and the original's SHA-256 was unchanged.
- **ASS** (Korra S01E01's Greek lines made into a fansub-like ASS: 372 dialogue events, every tenth line drawn on two layers, some at the top of the screen, 15 signs with `\pos` in a Signs style listed after the dialogue, 3 karaoke lines in an OP Romaji style, 6 comments, three styles, a byte-order mark and mixed line endings). Its dialogue scrambled as above; the signs, songs and comments only by the delay and the drift, as a whole file from another release would be. Synced in 31 s: the 372 events read as 338 lines, 334 by their words. Against where the sync puts the same lines as SRT unscrambled: median 0.00 s, 98% within 0.2 s; every line's layers on the same times. The signs, 2.4 s off before, came within 0.9 s of their true times (the median 0.26 s, the lean of this file's own timing, above), the songs within 0.6 s, each keeping its length to the centisecond; the comments untouched; every byte but the times identical. The same as a track of a film (with a font attached), from the app: the same results; saved, the copy's ASS track was the synced file byte for byte, the font and the picture and sound identical, the original unchanged. Added as a file, the same file came out byte for byte as when synced directly.
- **A whole file 60 s late** (2026-11-27: Sicario's Greek SRT with every line 60 s later, and a credit at 0:05 nobody speaks): 869 of its 904 lines paired by their words; 901 of the 903 dialogue lines within 0.1 s of the sync the owner approved, the other two within 2.3 s. Before, none was paired. Sicario itself, synced again, came out byte for byte as before. The credit, placed 50 s before the film starts, shows from 0:00 to 0:03, its own length, and nothing else moved; two nonsense lines were refused as matching nothing.
- **SRT unchanged by ASS**: Sicario synced again before and after, byte-identical (903 lines, 868 by their words, 35 placed between them, as the owner approved), and Korra's own SRT track byte-identical to its earlier sync. A sync is deterministic: the same lines and film give the same file.

## Limits

- **Subtitles in text only**: SRT, ASS and SSA files added to a film, and the film's own SRT, ASS, SSA, timed-text and WebVTT tracks. Subtitles made of pictures (PGS, VobSub) have no words to pair until they are read.
- **In ASS, what isn't dialogue is known by its tags, effect and style name.** A sign in a style named like dialogue, with no `\pos`, is taken for dialogue: with no speech to pair, it is placed by its neighbours. A sign or song can only be as exact as the dialogue around it: within about half a second where each line is off by up to 2 s of its own.
- **Speech in 11 languages** is timed (Qwen3-ForcedAligner's: English, Chinese, Cantonese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish). A sound track tagged with another (Greek) is refused with that said. The subtitles may be in any language BGE-M3 reads.
- **One sync at a time**: a season's episodes are asked for together, and sync one after another (see [A season, and taking turns](#a-season-and-taking-turns)). The models are loaded afresh for each file.
- Lines nobody speaks (signs, credits) can only be placed by their neighbours.
- **The sync's own timing convention**: a line starts on its first word. Subtitles that started lines early by habit (Korra S01E01's Greek, by 0.24 s) come out on the words instead.
- **Tested on one card.** A sync holds 8.35 GB of the card at its peak, and CUDA about half a gigabyte more: more than an 8 GB card, the download's least, has. There it would run on memory NVIDIA's driver lends from the PC, much slower, or fail. No smaller card was tried.

## Choices

- **Speech recognition, not subtitle matching.** Most films the owner syncs have no other subtitles, and matching lines against another language's subtitles by timing alone could not tell which line was which where the two break dialogue differently (tried 2026-11-26: every fix for one spot broke another).
- **Nothing copied from other projects.** sc0ty's subsync (GPL, fits one straight line through all of a file, so it cannot fix lines off in different directions) and Anchor (AGPL: WhisperX and NLLB translation; it got 2 of the owner's 7 spots right on Sicario, its misses being lines it could not match by word and guessed) were studied; the pipeline here is written from scratch.
- **Permissive models only**: Apache-2.0 and MIT. NLLB, which Anchor uses, is CC-BY-NC.
- **The largest models the card runs easily** rather than the fastest: Qwen3-ASR 1.7B over 0.6B, the aligner over Whisper's own word times. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (30B) was too large for 16 GB.
- **A private Python rather than the user's.** Other users won't all have one, and one the user upgrades or removes would break the sync.

## Testing

Drive the dev app over the DevTools protocol (see [decisions.md](decisions.md#testing-notes)). `subsync_status`, `subsync_install` and `subsync_remove` can be invoked directly; the dev build's download is `%LOCALAPPDATA%\app.siphon.desktop.dev\subsync\`. To check timing without watching, scramble a correctly timed SRT (a delay, a drift, each line moved by up to ±2 s) and compare each line's start with the original's, pairing lines by their text. To mux a scrambled track into a film, keep its lines in their order (hand the moved times out in order): Matroska stores lines by time, so lines moved past each other come out reordered, which real subtitles never are and which pairing in order can't undo (tried: 79% of lines within 0.2 s, against 97% kept in order). Measure against where the sync puts the unscrambled subtitles too: a file's own timing may follow another sound track, or start lines early by habit. Work on hard links or copies of media in a scratch folder: the copy's `Edited` folder is made beside the film.
